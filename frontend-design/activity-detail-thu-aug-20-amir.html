Why Nepal?

The World's Greatest Trekking Destination

Nepal is home to eight of the world's fourteen 8,000-meter peaks, offering an unparalleled diversity of landscapes within a relatively small area. Trekking here isn't just about mountains; it's a journey through ancient cultures, terraced farmlands, and rhododendron forests that burst into color each spring.

Whether you're seeking a challenging expedition to Everest Base Camp or a serene walk through the Annapurna foothills, our network of teahouses and experienced guides ensures every step is safe, comfortable, and deeply memorable.

Understanding Grades

Our treks range from gentle cultural walks to strenuous high-altitude expeditions.

Easy (1-7 days) Moderate (8-14 days) Challenging (15+ days) Strenuous (High Pass/Expedition)

Medical & Fitness Prerequisites

You don't need to be an elite athlete, but good cardiovascular health is essential. We recommend starting training at least 2 months prior with regular hiking, stair climbing, or running. Consult your physician regarding altitude medication (Diamox) and ensure your insurance covers evacuation up to 6,000m.

Landscape Diversity

Nepal's terrain rises from near sea level to 8,848m within 200km. Expect subtropical jungles in the lower foothills, terraced rice paddies in the mid-hills, dense rhododendron and pine forests, and finally the rugged alpine moraine and glaciers of the high Himalaya.

Communities of the Trail

Trekking in Nepal is as much about people as peaks. Encounter Sherpa Buddhism in the east, Gurung and Magar traditions in the central hills, and ancient Bon practices in Mustang. Always ask permission before photographing locals and respect sacred mani stones and chortens by passing them clockwise.

Daily Life on the Trail

Typical days start early with tea around 6 AM. Walking begins by 7-8 AM with frequent breaks. Lunch is usually served between 11 AM-1 PM at a trailside lodge. Afternoons are shorter, arriving at tonight's village by 3-4 PM for tea, rest, and dinner around 6-7 PM. Accommodation is in basic but cozy teahouses with communal dining halls.

Common Questions

Trekking FAQs

Is trekking in Nepal safe for solo travelers?

Yes, Nepal is generally very safe. However, we strongly recommend hiring a licensed guide for navigation, safety monitoring, and cultural insight. Solo trekking without a guide is now restricted in many national parks.

Do I need previous trekking experience?

Not for Easy or Moderate graded treks. For Challenging or Strenuous routes like EBC or Manaslu Circuit, prior multi-day hiking experience is beneficial but not mandatory if you are physically fit.

What happens if I get sick on the trail?

Our guides are trained in wilderness first aid and carry medical kits including oximeters. In case of serious illness or AMS, emergency helicopter evacuation protocols are immediately activated (covered by your mandatory insurance).
