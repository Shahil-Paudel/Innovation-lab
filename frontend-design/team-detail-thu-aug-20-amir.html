From Namche to the World

Pasang's story is the story of the Khumbu itself — one written in footsteps, prayer flags, and the rhythm of the seasons.

"The mountain doesn't care how fast you climb. It cares that you arrive whole."

Philosophy

Slow is smooth. Smooth is safe. Safe is how you come home with the best photographs — and the best memories.

A Life Measured in Mountain Passes

Pasang Sherpa was twelve years old when he carried his first trekker's daypack up the trail from Phakding to Namche Bazaar. It was 2008, the autumn season, and the rhododendron forest was still green at the lower elevations. He remembers the weight of that pack — and the weight of the responsibility he felt when the trekker, a retired teacher from Lyon, stumbled on the stones above the Dudh Koshi and Pasang steadied him with a hand on the shoulder. That small moment, he says, is when he knew he wanted to guide for a living.

Today, eighteen years and more than four hundred treks later, Pasang is one of the most experienced senior guides at Sagarmatha Treks. He holds the IFMGA International Mountain Guide certification — one of fewer than a dozen Nepali guides to do so — and maintains a current Wilderness First Responder credential. He has summited Everest twice (without supplemental oxygen on the second ascent), led expeditions to Ama Dablam and Island Peak, and guided the Manaslu Circuit more times than he can comfortably count.

How he guides

Pasang's style is quiet, observant, and deeply personal. He learns your name on day one and your pace by day two. He notices when someone's water bottle is running low before they do. He knows which teahouse in Dingboche has the best dal bhat, which corner of Tengboche Monastery catches the late afternoon light, and when to suggest an extra rest day — even when the group insists they're fine.

His approach to altitude is conservative by design. Twice-daily oximeter checks are non-negotiable on his trips. Acclimatization days are treated as part of the itinerary, not optional extras. "The mountain doesn't care how fast you climb," he says. "It cares that you arrive whole." That philosophy has earned him a 4.96 average rating across more than 280 verified reviews.

Beyond the trail

When he's not in the hills, Pasang runs the Sagarmatha Guide Training Program in Kathmandu, mentoring the next generation of licensed guides. He's also an active member of the Khumbu Environmental Committee, helping design the waste-management protocols now used across the national park. He lives in Boudha with his wife, two daughters, and a very patient golden retriever named Tenzing.

What Pasang Does Best

Specialties & Skills

Every guide has a strength. These are the areas where Pasang is the person you want on your team.

High Altitude Rescue

Trained in helicopter-evac coordination, GAMOW bag deployment, and advanced field triage above 5,000 m. First-responder on three Khumbu rescues in the last five seasons.

Cultural Interpretation

Born into Sherpa culture, Pasang translates monastery rituals, mani stones and prayer-flag symbolism into stories that make the Khumbu feel alive — not just scenic.

Mountain Photography

Knows every sunrise vantage point between Lukla and Gorak Shep. Will help you frame the shot — and then hand you back your phone so you can enjoy the view yourself.

Bird & Wildlife Watching

Can spot a Himalayan monal at 200 paces and identify the call of a blood pheasant before you hear it. Keeps a small field guide in his daypack at all times.

First-Timer Mentorship

Specialist in easing nervous first-timers through their inaugural Himalayan trek. Patient, encouraging, and expert at pacing a group with mixed fitness levels.

Private Itinerary Design

Co-designs custom routes for families, photographers, and experienced trekkers who want something beyond the standard circuit. Acclimatization built around your days.
