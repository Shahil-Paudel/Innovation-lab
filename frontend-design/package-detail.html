Overview

About the Everest Base Camp Trek

"Can I actually do this?"The question we hear most in our Thamel office

After guiding hundreds of trekkers a year through the Khumbu — every age, every fitness level — our honest answer is yes, if you can walk 5 to 7 hours a day on uneven ground and you take altitude seriously. No technical climbing is involved; the mountain asks for endurance and patience, not ropes.

You won't stand on the 8,848 m summit — that's an expedition. What you will do is stand at Everest Base Camp (5,364 m) beside the Khumbu Icefall, and climb Kala Patthar (5,555 m) the next morning for the photograph you came for.

Base Camp itself sits below the Icefall, where the summit hides behind Nuptse. Kala Patthar is the view — and our guides time the climb for sunrise or late afternoon depending on that week's cloud pattern, because they still photograph it themselves every trip.

Why 14 days? Two acclimatization nights — Namche Bazaar and Dingboche — plus a buffer for Lukla weather. The pacing is the product: it's the difference between surviving the trek and enjoying it.

The trail follows the Dudh Koshi from Lukla through pine forest and suspension bridges to Phakding, then climbs 800 m to Namche. At Tengboche the forest opens to alpine country and Everest finally shows himself; beyond Dingboche the moraine of the Khumbu Glacier leads to Base Camp, and the quieter return drops via Pheriche.

Expect stone staircases around Lukla, microspikes in snowy conditions, and Everest visibility that comes and goes until Gorak Shep. Read our guides on the best season to trek and what to pack, then walk it hour by hour in the day-by-day itinerary below.

Everest Base Camp Trek Itinerary

Rest days at Namche & Dingboche are non-negotiable. Our tip: keep 2–3 buffer days in Kathmandu after the trek — Lukla weather decides your flight home.

Private transfer to your Thamel hotel; evening trek briefing and gear check with your guide.

A 25-minute mountain flight over the Himalaya, then a gentle first walk along the Dudh Koshi through pine and prayer flags.

Cross the Hillary Bridge and climb 800 m through forest to the Sherpa capital, Everest teasing through the pines.

Climb high, sleep low: a ridge hike to the world's highest luxury hotel and the Tenzing Norgay statue, coffee above the clouds.

Rhododendron forest opens to alpine views; catch the evening puja at the Khumbu's greatest monastery, Ama Dablam towering behind.

Past Pangboche's ancient gompa into stone-walled potato fields; the treeline falls away and the mountains close in.

A steady ridge hike with views of Makalu and Island Peak. Climb high, sleep low, hydrate, repeat.

A quiet pause at the Thukla Pass memorials, then moraine trails beside the Khumbu Glacier into high-alpine country.

The big day: rocky moraine to the prayer flags and the Base Camp rock beside the Icefall; sleep at Gorak Shep (5,164 m).

Pre-dawn climb for the classic Everest panorama, breakfast at Gorak Shep, then a long satisfying descent to Pheriche.

A surprisingly fast return on warm, oxygen-rich trails — celebrate with a bakery stop in Namche.

The final stroll down the Dudh Koshi; thank your crew and swap trail stories over a last teahouse dinner.

Morning flight out of the mountains; a free afternoon for souvenirs or a well-earned massage in Thamel.

Private transfer to the airport three hours before your flight — certificate in hand, camera full, heart fuller.

Everest Base Camp Trek FAQs

Fitness & Difficulty

Yes — with proper preparation. The EBC trek requires endurance, not technical climbing. If you can walk 5 to 7 hours a day on uneven ground and take altitude seriously, first-timers complete it every season.

Good baseline fitness with the ability to walk sustained hours. Prepare with regular walking, light cardio and hill work — consistency matters more than intensity.

Altitude, not terrain. Above 3,500 m oxygen levels drop, and long walking days compound fatigue. Our pacing and acclimatization nights manage both.

Best Time & Weather

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) give the clearest skies, most reliable Lukla flights and most comfortable daytime temperatures. Autumn in particular produces the sharpest mountain views.

Both are possible. Winter is colder with icy trails; monsoon brings cloud cover and a higher chance of Lukla delays. Both seasons require more schedule flexibility.

Delays are normal, even in peak season. We include a buffer day and keep your international connections protected. Early morning flights are most reliable.

Altitude & Safety

Mild symptoms are common above 3,000 m; serious cases are rare with proper pacing. Our twice-daily oximeter checks catch trends early, and descent is always the first response.

A policy covering high-altitude trekking to 6,000 m plus emergency helicopter evacuation is mandatory. Arrange it before you fly to Nepal — many providers won't issue cover after arrival.

Cost & Booking

A 20% deposit confirms your place; the balance is payable on arrival in Kathmandu, by cash or card (card carries a 3.5% bank fee). Flight details can be added later.

Yes. The deposit is non-refundable but becomes a lifetime credit toward any future trek with us — no expiry, no paperwork.

Accommodation & Food

Locally run lodges with twin-share rooms, simple bedding, and a warm dining hall that's the social heart of each village. Lower villages have attached bathrooms; higher ones share facilities.

Warm, filling and designed for long walking days — dal bhat, pasta, soups, noodle dishes. Above Namche, vegetarian meals are the safer choice. Vegan and gluten-free options with advance notice.

Gear & Luggage

One porter per two trekkers, carrying up to 25 kg total (~12–13 kg per person). You carry a small daypack. Lukla flights have a strict 15 kg limit including hand luggage.

A –15° down jacket, –20° sleeping bag and 70 L duffel at your Kathmandu briefing — included, no rental fee. Returned at the end of the trek.
